# Blog Post Plan — `albania-investment-by-budget-and-goal`

## Title
What Your Budget Actually Buys in Albania, and Which Zone Fits Your Goal

## keyFacts
1. Durrës Plazh short-let apartments run at roughly 7-11% gross yield, the highest reliable short-let return we track in Albania.
2. Vlorë produces the best absolute short-let income in the country at around 1,163 euros a month on comparable stock, ahead of Sarandë.
3. For year-round long-let income the strongest zones are Tirana's Myslym Shyri and Don Bosko, carried by students, offices and steady demand rather than by season.
4. Under 50,000 euros the realistic options are Tale, Mali i Robit and Kombinat, and all three require legal checks before anything is signed.
5. Albanian transaction costs add roughly 3-4% on top of the purchase price, and rental income is taxed at 15%, so gross yield overstates what you keep by a wide margin.

## excerpt
Two questions decide an Albanian purchase, and neither is "which city is best". They are: what is the money for, and how much of it is there. This is our 2026 mapping of goal to zone across the country, followed by four worked allocations from 100,000 euros to a million. It is a framework for thinking, not investment advice, and the numbers carry the confidence levels we attach to them.

## Body
Most guides answer "where should I buy in Albania" with a city. That is the wrong granularity. Tirana contains both the best long-let income in the country and its least liquid luxury stock; Vlorë contains both an overheated seafront and undervalued city blocks. The zone matters, and so does what you want the money to do.

Everything below is our own assessment as of 2026 at moderate confidence, and the model allocations at the end are illustrative rather than advice. We are a listings platform, not a licensed adviser, and you should treat our view accordingly.

#### Match the zone to the goal

| Goal | First choice | Second choice | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-let income, year round | Tirana: Myslym Shyri, Don Bosko | Vlorë city; Durrës Currila | Students, offices and port employment rather than season |
| Short-let income | Durrës Plazh, 7-11% gross | Vlorë, best absolute income; central Sarandë | The south needs active management to work |
| Family living | Tirana: Komuna e Parisit, Sauk | Vlorë Uji i Ftohtë; Durrës Currila | Schools, parks, year-round life |
| Retirement | Vlorë, city plus sea plus hospitals | Sarandë, milder winter, weaker medicine | Winter liveability is the deciding factor |
| Digital nomads | Tirana, around Blloku | Sarandë in winter; Vlorë | Coworking exists in Tirana and effectively nowhere else |
| Resale in three to five years | Qerret; second line of the New Boulevard | The rail corridor | Only worth it if you enter at a discount |
| Capital preservation, ten years plus | Prime Tirana: Blloku, the lake, the embassy quarter | Lalzi Bay luxury | Scarcity plus the only reliable liquidity in the country |
| First purchase by a foreign buyer | Vlorë city or second-line Lungomare | Durrës Qerret | Exit to diaspora and European buyers is realistic |
| Under 50,000 euros | Tale, Mali i Robit, Kombinat | Older Sarandë stock, with quality caveats | Viable only with legal checks done first |

Two entries deserve expanding, because they are the ones people get wrong most often.

**Short-let is not a coastal default.** Durrës Plazh delivers the best gross yield we track, at 7-11%, because it draws domestic and regional visitors across a longer season and sits an hour from the airport. Vlorë produces the highest absolute monthly income, around 1,163 euros. Sarandë and Ksamil are more famous and, on current prices, worse trades. And on the southern coast none of this works without professional management — an unmanaged unit in Sarandë is a fixed cost with a seasonal trickle of income.

**Retirement is decided in January, not August.** Vlorë wins because it is a functioning year-round city with hospitals. Sarandë has the milder winter and the weaker medical infrastructure. Anyone choosing between them on a summer visit is choosing on the wrong evidence.

#### Four allocations, worked through

These are illustrative portfolios showing how the zones above combine. They are not recommendations, and the return expectations attach to our base scenario, which we hold at roughly 50-55% probability.

| Budget | Allocation | Base-case expectation |
|---|---|---|
| 100,000 euros | A one-bedroom in Durrës Plazh or Qerret for short-let at about 75,000, with the balance held for renovation and furnishing | 6-9% gross, plus 5-10% a year on capital |
| 250,000 euros | A two-bedroom in Tirana Don Bosko for long-let at about 120,000, plus a one-bedroom on Vlorë's second line for short-let at about 110,000 | City and coast diversified; roughly 5-7% blended |
| 500,000 euros | Prime Tirana two-bedroom at about 250,000, two Qerret units at about 180,000, and cash held back | Capital plus income, with an option on a discounted deal |
| 1,000,000 euros and above | A villa in Lalzi Bay or Farkë at about 600,000, a prime apartment at about 300,000, and land with clean title | Preservation, with a long horizon on European convergence |

Notice what the larger allocations do: they hold cash. In a market where transactions are down about 35% year on year, the ability to move quickly on a motivated seller is worth more than being fully invested.

#### The costs that turn gross into net

Gross yield is the number in the advertisement. It is not the number you keep.

- Transaction costs add roughly 3-4% to the purchase price.
- Rental income is taxed at 15%.
- Management, on the coast, is not optional, and it is not free.
- Furnishing a short-let unit to a competitive standard is a real capital cost, which is why the 100,000 euro allocation above holds money back for it.

A 9% gross short-let yield in Durrës is a materially smaller number after all four. It is still a good return by European standards. It is simply not 9%.

#### Where we would start

If you are making a first purchase and want one sentence: a completed, certified two-bedroom in Vlorë city or Durrës, bought with the transaction costs and the 15% tax modelled from the outset, is a more reliable outcome than anything on the Riviera at 2026 prices.

## faq
**What is a realistic rental yield in Albania?**
Short-let in Durrës Plazh runs at roughly 7-11% gross, which is the strongest reliable return we track, and Vlorë produces the highest absolute monthly income at around 1,163 euros. Those are gross figures. After roughly 3-4% transaction costs, 15% tax on rental income, management and furnishing, the number you keep is considerably lower — still competitive by European standards, but not the headline.

**How much do I need to invest in Albanian property?**
Entry starts below 50,000 euros in Tale, Mali i Robit and Kombinat, though at that level legal checks matter more than location. Around 100,000 euros buys a one-bedroom short-let unit in Durrës Plazh or Qerret with a reserve for furnishing. Above 250,000 you can diversify between city long-let and coastal short-let rather than depending on one asset.

**Which Albanian city is best for rental income?**
It depends on the type of letting. For year-round long-let, Tirana's Myslym Shyri and Don Bosko, because students, offices and steady employment do not have a season. For short-let, Durrës Plazh on gross yield and Vlorë on absolute income. Sarandë and Ksamil are better known and, at current prices, weaker on the arithmetic.

**Where should a retiree buy in Albania?**
Vlorë, on our assessment, because it functions as a city year-round and has hospitals. Sarandë has a milder winter but weaker medical infrastructure, which matters more with time. The common mistake is deciding after a summer visit, when both look equally good and neither is being tested.
