# Blog Post Plan — `where-not-to-buy-albania-2026`

## Title
Where We Would Not Buy in Albania in 2026, and Why

## keyFacts
1. Ksamil now trades at a higher price per square metre than Sarandë while producing lower short-let income, and new supply there has risen sharply.
2. Foreign demand on the southern coast has fallen for two consecutive years — around 50% and then a further 30% in Sarandë — while asking prices kept climbing.
3. Prime Sarandë seafront and resort stock at roughly 3,000-4,000 euros per square metre carries water and sewerage constraints, and the long-promised Sarandë airport does not exist.
4. Tirana luxury towers at roughly 5,000-10,000 euros per square metre yield under 3%, and their resale liquidity is unproven.
5. Part of the premium paid in Vlorë and on the southern coast assumes Vlorë airport opens; the airport has not opened, and each delay erodes that premium.

## excerpt
Most Albanian property content tells you where to buy. This one is the other half of the job. These are the seven zones where, on our own reading of the 2026 data, the price has run ahead of what the fundamentals support, and the specific symptom in each case. None of this is advice to avoid a place permanently, and none of it means a good unit cannot be bought well.

## Body
An agent is paid to sell you what they have. We publish listings too, so treat this piece with the scepticism it deserves — and then check the numbers, because they are checkable.

What follows is our assessment as of 2026 of the Albanian zones where price has outrun fundamentals. Confidence here is moderate, not high: these are judgements built on price data, rental income, permit volumes and demand trends, not certainties. A zone appearing below does not mean nothing there is worth owning. It means the average asking price already assumes a good outcome, so you are paying for the upside rather than buying it.

#### The pattern to look for

Across all seven, one shape repeats: the price embeds an event that has not happened. An airport that has not opened. A port that has not moved. Infrastructure that has been announced. When the event is already in the price, you carry the risk of delay without being paid for it.

That is the test we would apply to any zone, including ones not on this list.

#### Ksamil

Ksamil sells the most photographed water in Albania, and it is priced accordingly. The problem is that its price per square metre now exceeds Sarandë's while its short-let income runs lower — roughly 5,500 US dollars against about 7,000 for Sarandë on comparable stock. That is the wrong way round for an investment case built on rental yield.

New supply has also grown by a large margin, which puts further pressure on both occupancy and resale. Add the legal questions that have attached to construction near the dunes and inside protected national park boundaries, and the risk-adjusted picture is weak at current asking prices.

#### Sarandë seafront and resort stock

The headline number here is demand, not price. Foreign buyer demand in Sarandë fell by around half, then by a further 30% the following year. Prices rose anyway. Two years of falling demand against rising prices is the signature of a late-cycle market being held up by seller expectations rather than by buyers.

Prime seafront and resort units at roughly 3,000-4,000 euros per square metre also depend on infrastructure that has limits: water supply and sewerage are genuine constraints in season. And the "Sarandë airport" that appears in sales conversations is not a project with an opening date.

#### Vlorë Lungomare and Akërnia

Vlorë has doubled in three years in parts. A large share of that is an airport premium — the expectation that Vlorë International will open and bring direct European traffic. The airport is a real project. It is also not open, and its timeline has moved.

If it opens on schedule, today's prices are defensible. If it slips again, you have paid for it twice.

#### Tirana luxury towers

The capital's top-end towers at roughly 5,000-10,000 euros per square metre have two problems. The first is arithmetic: gross yields under 3% do not compensate for the risk of owning an illiquid asset in a small market. The second is observable — commentary on the Tirana skyline has repeatedly noted how many of these units sit empty.

Empty units are not automatically a crisis; second homes and stores of value are legitimate. But they are a warning about exit. You need a buyer with the same motivation to get out at your price, and that buyer pool is small.

#### Durrës Marina

The marina district is the most aggressively marketed development on the central coast. Our reservations are about structure rather than location: reports of settlement in some blocks, a sales model that leans heavily on pre-sales funding later phases, and a port relocation that underpins much of the pitch and has not happened.

Pre-sale-funded development is normal. It is also the model that leaves buyers exposed when volumes fall — and volumes are falling.

#### Paskuqan and Kamëz new build at around 1,200 euros

These northern Tirana areas have seen new build asking prices converge on what Astir commands. The two are not comparable. Title risk is materially higher, and the social and transport infrastructure is thinner. Paying an Astir price for a Paskuqan title is the clearest example on this list of a price that has simply detached.

#### Rana e Hedhur, Shëngjin

Shëngjin's northern frontier carries a "last undeveloped beach" premium. It also carries live legal proceedings concerning construction on the dunes, and documented coastal erosion. Where a legal process is unresolved, the honest position is that the risk is unpriced rather than that the property is bad — but unpriced risk at a premium price is a poor combination.

#### What to do with this list

Three things, in order.

- Ask what event the price assumes, and get the current status of that event in writing.
- Compare the price per square metre to the rent the unit actually earns today, not to what a neighbouring zone charges.
- Check the title and the permit before anything else, especially near dunes, protected areas and reclassified agricultural land.

If a zone here still looks right for you after that, buy it — with a discount that reflects what you have just found. Our objection is almost never to the place. It is to the price the place is being sold at in 2026.

## faq
**Is Ksamil a bad investment?**
Not inherently, but at 2026 asking prices the arithmetic is unfavourable. Ksamil trades above Sarandë per square metre while generating lower short-let income — roughly 5,500 US dollars against about 7,000 on comparable stock — and new supply has risen sharply, which pressures both occupancy and resale. There are also unresolved legal questions around building near the dunes and inside protected boundaries.

**Why is falling demand in Sarandë not showing up in prices?**
Because asking prices reflect what sellers hope to get, and they adjust slowly. Foreign demand in Sarandë fell around 50% and then a further 30% across two consecutive years while prices continued to rise. That gap between demand and price is typically closed by a long period of very few transactions rather than by a visible crash, which is why buyers should expect to negotiate hard rather than wait for a headline correction.

**Should I avoid the Albanian coast altogether in 2026?**
No. The concern is specific zones at specific prices, not the coast as a category. Parts of the coast remain reasonably valued — Vlorë's ordinary city stock and Qerret, for example, look better on fundamentals than the resort segment. The rule that matters is to avoid paying today for infrastructure that has only been announced.

**How can I check these claims myself?**
Compare the asking price per square metre with the actual achieved rent for the same building, ask the agent for the current legal status of any airport, port or road the pitch relies on, and have a lawyer confirm the title and the construction permit before you pay a deposit. Every number in this article comes from published market data, and every one of them can be re-checked at the time you buy.
