# Blog Post Plan — `undervalued-areas-albania-2026`

## Title
Seven Albanian Areas Where the Price Has Not Caught Up With the Drivers

## keyFacts
1. The Tirana-Durrës rail link will cut the journey to about 22 minutes, and the areas along it - Shkozet, Laprakë and the Kashar stations - do not yet price that in.
2. Qerret sits at roughly 1,100-2,000 euros per square metre while growing 21-43% year on year, below first-line Golem despite comparable access to the beach.
3. Vlorë's ordinary city stock at around 800-1,000 euros per square metre has been left behind by the Lungomare hype in what has become a two-speed local market.
4. Kombinat in Tirana trades at roughly 1,000-1,300 euros per square metre with early-stage gentrification and a real neighbourhood identity.
5. Tale and the southern Lezhë coast start from about 600 euros per square metre, the cheapest coastal entry point in the country, with the Milot-Balldren road due by 2029.

## excerpt
The counterpart to knowing where not to buy is knowing where the price has not yet absorbed something that is already happening. These are seven Albanian areas where, on our reading of 2026 data, a driver exists and the market has not fully repriced for it. The entry points are lower and so is the marketing budget, which is usually the point.

## Body
Undervalued does not mean cheap. Plenty of Albanian property is cheap because nobody wants it, and it will stay that way. Undervalued means there is an identifiable driver — a road, a rail line, a shift in who is buying — that the current price does not reflect.

Our confidence here is moderate. These are judgements, and the biggest single risk to all of them is the same: infrastructure timelines in Albania slip. Where a thesis depends on a project completing, we say so.

#### The rail corridor: Shkozet, Laprakë and the Kashar stations

The Tirana-Durrës railway reduces the journey between the two largest urban markets to roughly 22 minutes. That is the difference between two cities and one commuter region.

Property immediately around the planned stations has not repriced for this the way comparable corridors elsewhere do. Our base expectation is 5-10% a year in these pockets once services actually run — with the obvious caveat that "once services actually run" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

#### Qerret

Qerret trades at roughly 1,100-2,000 euros per square metre while posting 21-43% annual growth, and it is drawing European buyers directly. It sits beside Golem, whose first line commands more for a similar beach.

The thesis is simple convergence: there is no durable reason for the gap to stay this wide. This is the area on the list where we would expect the shortest wait.

#### Vlorë's ordinary city stock

Vlorë has become two markets. The Lungomare seafront and Akërnia carry an airport premium and have doubled in three years. Meanwhile the ordinary city — where people actually live year-round — sits at roughly 800-1,000 euros per square metre.

The local segment has been ignored because it is not what gets marketed to foreign buyers. It also has what the seafront does not: year-round tenants, hospitals, schools and a functioning winter. For rental income rather than a holiday asset, the second line is the better trade.

#### Kombinat, Tirana

At roughly 1,000-1,300 euros per square metre, Kombinat is one of the last Tirana districts with a distinct identity and an early-stage gentrification pattern rather than a completed one. The comparison we would draw is Don Bosko, which made the same transition earlier and now costs more.

This is a five-year thesis, not a two-year one.

#### Orikum

Orikum runs at roughly 800-1,500 euros per square metre with marinas and the Riviera immediately south. The lower end of the market has already moved sharply, up around 63%, which suggests the repricing has started rather than that it is finished.

It depends on marina development continuing. If that stalls, so does this.

#### Tale and the southern Lezhë coast

From about 600 euros per square metre, this is the cheapest way onto the Albanian coast. The driver is the Milot-Balldren road, expected by 2029.

We would call this speculative and say so plainly. The horizon is long, the infrastructure is not built, and the area has little else supporting it today. It belongs in a portfolio as a small position, not as a first purchase.

#### Village resale around Dhërmi and Himarë

Qeparo and Vuno resale sits at roughly 1,100-1,800 euros per square metre. The driver is the Vlorë valley road, which opens a second route onto the Riviera and is not in the price.

One serious warning attaches to this one specifically. Title quality in the Riviera villages is the weakest of anything on this list — restitution claims, unclear inheritance, agricultural classification. Do not buy here without a lawyer who has done Riviera title work before, and expect some properties to be unbuyable at any price.

#### How to use a list like this

The honest summary is that every entry above is a bet on timing. The drivers are real and documented; the dates are not in your control.

That argues for two rules. Buy where the driver has already begun rather than where it has only been announced — Qerret and Vlorë city over Tale, on that test. And size the position to the risk: a speculative coastal plot is not the place for the whole budget.

If you want the other side of this analysis, we also publish the zones where we think the price has run ahead of the fundamentals.

## faq
**What does undervalued mean here?**
It means an identifiable driver exists that the current price does not reflect — a rail line, a road, a shift in the buyer pool — not simply that the price is low. Cheap property with no driver stays cheap. Every area in this article is paired with the specific driver behind it, so the thesis can be checked and, if the driver fails, abandoned.

**Which of these is the safest entry?**
On our reading, Qerret and Vlorë's ordinary city stock. Both have drivers that are already in motion rather than announced: Qerret is growing 21-43% a year with European buyers arriving, and Vlorë city has year-round tenants regardless of what happens at the airport. Tale and the Riviera villages carry the highest upside and by far the highest risk.

**What is the biggest risk to these areas?**
Infrastructure delay. The rail corridor, the Milot-Balldren road and the Vlorë valley route all underpin theses above, and Albanian project timelines have moved before. A secondary risk is title quality, which is a serious constraint in the Riviera villages in particular.

**Should I buy in an undervalued area or an established one?**
It depends on what you want from the purchase. Established zones such as prime Tirana offer liquidity and a shorter path to renting the unit out; undervalued zones offer more upside and demand patience plus a higher tolerance for legal work. Most buyers we speak to are better served by one established position first, with a smaller speculative one only after the first is working.
