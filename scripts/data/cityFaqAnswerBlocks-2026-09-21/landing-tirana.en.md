## faq-1-b29be2
> Which areas of Tirana are the most expensive?
Blloku is the most expensive district: new-build flats ask €3,000–5,500/m² and resale €2,500–3,500. Next come the Artificial Lake area at €2,400–3,000 for new build, where one luxury tower set the national record of €10,000/m², and the centre at €2,300–3,500. For scale, the average transaction price in the city was €1,863/m² in the second quarter of 2025, and €2,054 inside the Small Ring.

## faq-2-7d849a
> Is buying property in Tirana for investment favorable in 2026?
In 2026 Tirana is a buyer's market, not a rising one. The Bank of Albania index was flat in the second half of 2025 (0% on the half-year, +4.4% on the year), the time to sell rose from 7.1 to 8.1 months, and a record 1.9 million m² of permits issued in 2024 is still to be built. Gross yield on an annual let is about 4–6% by our estimate. District by goal: [the 2026 matrix](/blog/tirana-investment-area-2026).

## faq-3-a9e54b
> Which areas of Tirana are best for family living?
Komuna e Parisit, Myslym Shyri, Sauk and Liqeni i Thatë: parks, schools and quiet streets. New build asks €2,000–3,000/m² in Komuna e Parisit and €2,000–2,500 in Myslym Shyri; resale is €1,700–2,200 and €1,500–2,000. The city's international schools are the British School of Tirana, Tirana International School and GDQ in Don Bosko. The cost of the centre for a family is traffic and air quality. See [where to live in Tirana](/blog/best-districts-tirana).

## faq-4-304706
> How does proximity to the center and the sea affect prices?
Distance from the centre is the main price line: flats inside the Small Ring sold for €2,054/m² on average in the second quarter of 2025, against €1,768 just outside it, a gap of about 16%. Tirana itself is not on the sea. The nearest coast is Durrës, where a sea view has its own premium; what flats ask there, district by district, is in the [Durrës price table](/albania/durres/info).

## faq-6-b5fc2b
> How can I check if a property price is realistic?
Compare the price per m² with the same district in the table above, new build against new build and resale against resale: for example €1,500–2,000/m² for new build in Don Bosko and €1,200–1,500 in Astir. Then allow for negotiation: our sources put sales at 3–5% below the asking price in 2026. A flat far below its district's band needs an explanation, usually in the documents.

## faq-7-6212a0
> Can I buy property in Tirana without intermediaries?
Yes, an agent is not required, but a notary always is. A sale in Albania is valid only in notarial form: the notary holds the money in escrow, withholds the taxes and charges about 0.35% of the price. The customary agent commission is 2% from the seller and 1% from the buyer. A lawyer is not mandatory either, but checks the title, for €500–2,000. Read [buying in Tirana without an agent](/blog/tirana-buy-without-agent).

## faq-8-9cf121
> What documents are needed to buy property in Tirana?
From the seller: the ownership extract (kartela e pasurisë) from the cadastre ASHK, the statement of encumbrances, the cadastral map (harta treguese) and the history of the title; for a finished building, the occupancy permit (leje përdorimi). From you: a passport, and a sworn translator if you do not speak Albanian. Registration at the cadastre costs 5,000 lek, and with clean documents the purchase takes 4–8 weeks. See [how to buy property in Albania](/blog/buying-property-albania).

## faq-10-b69782
> Can I earn income from renting out property in Tirana?
Yes, at about 4–6% gross a year on an annual contract, by our estimate. Agency averages for the first half of 2026 were €590 a month for a one-bedroom flat and €954 for a two-bedroom; near the centre a one-bedroom asks €600–900, on the periphery €380–500. Rental income is taxed at a flat 15%. A one-bedroom let by the night full time is estimated at €10,717–11,718 gross a year, at 60–62% occupancy.

## faq-13-6f2d94
> Are there big differences between areas in price and popularity?
Yes, several times over per square metre. New-build flats ask €3,000–5,500/m² in Blloku, €1,500–2,000 in Don Bosko, €1,200–1,500 in Astir, €1,000–1,300 in Kombinat, €800–1,200 in Paskuqan and €750–900 in Kamëz. The cheap end carries its own risks: a large supply of new flats in Astir, Yzberisht and Kashar, and title problems in Paskuqan and Kamëz. Compare the districts in the table above.

## faq-14-4fdd38
> How does city development affect property prices?
Visibly, street by street. On the New Boulevard (Bulevardi i Ri) the second line rose about 36% in a year, from €1,400 to €1,900/m², while the first line asks €2,700. The Tirana–Durrës railway is the driver for the western axis of Laprakë, Kashar and Astir. Development also works the other way: a record 1.9 million m² of permits in 2024 means more competing flats in Astir, Yzberisht and Kashar.
