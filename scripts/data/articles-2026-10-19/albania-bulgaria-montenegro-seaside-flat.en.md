## body
Which is cheaper for a flat by the sea: Albania, Bulgaria or Montenegro? On the figures that can be compared, Albania: about €1,720 per m² on average in 2025, against €2,557 for new flats in Montenegro in the second quarter of 2026. Bulgaria cannot be put in the same table, because its statistics office stopped publishing average prices per square metre in 2014; its index shows prices rising about 15% a year. Bulgaria, however, taxes rent and sale gains least and has used the euro since January 2026. Below, the three countries side by side.

## Who may buy

- **Albania.** EU citizens buy flats and houses on the same terms as Albanians; agricultural land can only be leased, for up to 99 years.
- **Bulgaria.** Foreigners may own buildings; EU citizens may also own land under the conditions of Bulgaria's accession treaty. Buyers of farmland generally have to have lived in Bulgaria for more than five years.
- **Montenegro.** Foreigners may buy flats and houses but, according to law-firm guides, not agricultural or forest land, land within a kilometre of the land border, or land on islands.

## Taxes

| | Albania | Bulgaria | Montenegro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying a resale flat | 2% transfer tax, owed by the seller | 0.1–3%, set by the municipality | 3% up to €150,000, 5% up to €500,000, 6% above |
| New build | no VAT on apartment sales | 20% VAT | 21% VAT on the first sale |
| Annual property tax | 0.05% of fiscal value | 0.01–0.45% of tax value | 0.25–1% of market value |
| Rent | 15% | 10% | 15% after costs |
| Sale | 15% of the gain | 10%; a home held over 3 years is exempt (one a year) | 15%; only the owner's main home is exempt |

For a buyer who wants to let the flat and sell it one day, Bulgaria's rates are the lowest of the three. For a buyer who wants the lowest purchase price and the lowest annual tax, Albania wins: 0.05% of the fiscal value is usually a few thousand lek a year.

## What else to weigh

- **Season.** In Sarandë a one-bedroom flat let all season runs at about 90% occupancy in August and about 35% in December; we hold no comparable figures for the Bulgarian and Montenegrin coasts.
- **Supply.** Bulgaria's coast added new supply quickly: Deloitte reports supply up 20–25% since the start of 2026. Sarandë's holiday listings grew by 62% in 2025.
- **Transactions.** Bulgaria's transactions fell 15% in a year to Q1 2026, according to Deloitte; Albania's fell about 35% in 2025 while prices rose.

## faq

**Is Albania cheaper than Bulgaria and Montenegro?**
Than Montenegro, clearly: €1,720 per m² on average in Albania in 2025 against €2,557 for new flats in Montenegro in mid-2026. Bulgaria publishes no official average price per m², so a precise comparison is not possible; its index rose about 15% in a year.

**Where is the tax on rent lowest?**
In Bulgaria, at 10%. Albania and Montenegro charge 15%; Montenegro first deducts costs, with a 30% flat rate or 50–70% for tourist lets. In Albania a holiday let is taxed after the platform's commission.

**Which country taxes the sale of a flat least?**
Bulgaria: 10% on the gain, and a home held more than three years is exempt. Albania and Montenegro tax the gain at 15%; Montenegro exempts only the owner's main home, and Albania has no holding-period exemption.

**Can a Pole buy a flat in Bulgaria, Montenegro and Albania?**
Yes in all three. In Bulgaria EU citizens may own buildings and, under conditions, land. In Montenegro foreigners may buy flats and houses but not farmland, forest or land near the land border. In Albania only farmland is closed.

**Which of these countries uses the euro?**
Bulgaria joined the euro area on 1 January 2026, and the lev stopped being legal tender on 1 February. Montenegro uses the euro without an agreement with the EU. Albania keeps the lek, though property prices are usually quoted in euros.
