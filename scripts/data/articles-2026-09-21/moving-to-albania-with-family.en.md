## body
Our research describes Ukrainian buyers in Albania as families who relocate rather than investors: they look at Vlorë, Durrës, Sarandë and Tirana, spend €50,000–150,000, want a residence permit with the flat, and worry most about two things, getting the payment through and getting the permit. This article takes those questions in the order a family meets them. We use our research base only, and where it has no verified fact we say so instead of filling the gap.

## What residence rules apply to a Ukrainian family?

The route our research documents is ownership. Under Article 84 of Law 79/2021 a property owner can apply for a residence permit; the law sets no minimum purchase value, and agencies describe entry in practice at around €75,000–150,000. The permit runs for one year and is renewed, and permanent residence is possible after 5 years. We hold this at medium confidence.

What we do not have: any verified rule specific to Ukrainian citizens, on entry, length of stay or family members joining the owner. Do not take that from a forum or from us. Ask a migration lawyer in Albania before you commit money, and ask specifically how the permit of the owner extends to a spouse and children. The figures of €100,000 and €300,000 quoted for an "investor permit" after Law 43/2025 have no official publication behind them that we could find. The legal side of buying as a foreigner is in [can foreigners buy property in Albania](/blog/can-foreigners-buy-real-estate-albania).

## Where are the schools?

In Tirana. The international schools in our research are the British School of Tirana, Tirana International School (QSI) and GDQ in the Don Bosko district, and crowd-sourced data puts an international school at about €6,700 a year per child. We have no verified information on international schools in Durrës, Vlorë or Sarandë, nor on how Albanian state schools take in children who do not speak Albanian.

That makes the school the first fork in the decision. If the children are to study in English, the family lives in Tirana or within reach of it. If they study online in a Ukrainian school, the coast is open. Ask each school directly about fees and places; the figure above is an average from a public database, not a price list.

## Which city suits family life?

Tirana first, then Vlorë or the city part of Durrës: that is how our research ranks them for a family, on schools, parks and whether the place lives all year. Resort strips such as Golem, Ksamil or Shëngjin are summer places, where a family would spend the winter among closed shutters.

- **Tirana.** The family districts are Komuna e Parisit, Myslym Shyri, Sauk and Liqeni i Thatë: parks, schools and quiet. The price of the capital is traffic and air: nitrogen dioxide exceeded the limit at 66.7% of monitoring points, and crossing the city takes about 45 minutes at peak. See [where to live in Tirana](/blog/best-districts-tirana).
- **Durrës.** The centre, Currila and Vollga are lived in all year by local families and by people who work in Tirana. The beach strip is a summer place. An electric train to Tirana in 22 minutes is planned from early 2027, after repeated delays.
- **Vlorë.** The one real city on the southern coast, with a port, students and winter life; Uji i Ftohtë is the family district our research names.
- **Sarandë.** About 35,000–40,000 people in winter, most flats empty and much of the seafront closed; the state hospital is weak and serious cases go to Vlorë, Tirana or Corfu.

The three larger cities are compared in [Tirana, Durrës or Vlorë](/blog/how-to-choose-tirana-durres-vlore), and winter on the coast in [what it is like to live on the Riviera](/blog/living-albanian-riviera).

## What does it cost to live there?

Rent is the large item and it differs by city several times over. A two-bedroom flat is advertised at about €954 a month on average in Tirana, and our working bands are €650–950 in its middle ring and €450–650 on the periphery; in the centre of Durrës and in inland Vlorë at €350–550; by the sea in Vlorë at €500–800; in Sarandë at €400–700. These are asking rents, and outside Tirana the samples are small.

Two more facts about renting. The deposit is usually 1–2 months' rent, a lease longer than a year is written and notarised, and notice is 30–90 days. And on the coast a flat let by the year in winter may be wanted back for tourists in summer, when beach rents in Durrës roughly double; get the twelve months in writing.

Utilities differ little between cities. Our estimate for a 60 m² flat in winter, with electricity, water, waste, internet and the building fee, is about €128 a month for three people and €145 for four in Durrës, and €124 and €139 in Tirana. Electricity costs 10.2 lek per kWh with VAT up to 700 kWh a month; there is no household gas network, so flats heat with air conditioning. Durrës has the most expensive water in the country, 120 lek per m³ with sewerage, and supply there and in Vlorë runs about 13 hours a day, so ask whether the building has a tank and a pump.

For scale: the average rent in Tirana, €721, is 74% of the average gross wage of €979. Albania is cheap to run a flat in, not cheap to rent in its capital. Our research holds no verified prices for food, transport or childcare, so we give none.

## What do we know about healthcare?

Only where the hospitals are. In Tirana our research lists the Mother Teresa university hospital, the American Hospital and Continental; it counts Vlorë's hospitals among the reasons to choose that city, and records Sarandë's state hospital as weak. It holds no verified facts on how foreign residents enter the public system or on insurance, so arrange cover before you move and ask the migration lawyer what the permit requires.

## What documents do we need to buy?

Your passports, and a set of documents from the seller that you should obtain yourself rather than accept as copies: the ownership extract (kartela e pasurisë) from the cadastre ASHK, the statement of encumbrances, the cadastral map (harta treguese) and the history of the title. For a finished building ask for the occupancy permit (leje përdorimi). The full list is in our [document checklist](/blog/durres-documents-checklist).

How the purchase runs:

- A sale is valid only in notarial form; a private contract is void. The notary holds the money in escrow and withholds the taxes.
- If you do not speak Albanian, a sworn translator is mandatory and co-signs the deed.
- A lawyer is not required by law but is the person who checks the title; fees run €500–2,000. Registration at the cadastre costs 5,000 lek, the notary about 0.35%, and everything together roughly 3–4% of the price.
- With clean documents the whole cycle takes 4–8 weeks, and a power of attorney is possible.
- Payment: you can open an Albanian bank account, but banks check the source of funds strictly and it takes 1–4 weeks. It is not required: the price can go by SWIFT to the notary's escrow account.
- A mortgage is close to unavailable for a non-resident. What developers offer instead is payment by instalments at 0% until the keys, which carries the risk of paying for a building that is not finished.

## faq
**Can a Ukrainian family get a residence permit in Albania by buying a flat?**
Ownership is a recognised ground: Article 84 of Law 79/2021 lets a property owner apply for a residence permit, with no minimum value in the law, for one year at a time, and permanent residence is possible after 5 years. We hold no verified rule specific to Ukrainian citizens or on how the permit extends to a spouse and children, so confirm both with a migration lawyer.

**Are there international schools in Albania outside Tirana?**
Our research knows of none. The schools it lists are all in Tirana: the British School of Tirana, Tirana International School and GDQ, with crowd-sourced data putting the cost at about €6,700 a year. We have no verified information for Durrës, Vlorë or Sarandë, so a family that needs an English-language school should plan on living in Tirana.

**How much does it cost to rent a family flat in Albania?**
A two-bedroom flat is advertised at about €954 a month on average in Tirana, €350–550 in the centre of Durrës and in inland Vlorë, €500–800 by the sea in Vlorë and €400–700 in Sarandë. These are asking rents from small samples. The deposit is usually 1–2 months, and utilities for three people add an estimated €124–128 a month in winter.

**Which Albanian city is best for a family with children?**
Our research ranks Tirana first, in the districts of Komuna e Parisit and Sauk, for schools and parks, then Uji i Ftohtë in Vlorë and Currila in Durrës, which are lived in all year. Tirana's cost is rent, traffic and air quality. Sarandë is quiet in winter, with about 35,000–40,000 residents and a weak state hospital.
