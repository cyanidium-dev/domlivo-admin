## body
If you are retiring from Germany, Austria or Switzerland and thinking of a flat by the Adriatic, the running costs will not be your problem: by our estimate a flat for two costs about €100 a month to keep. What decides whether the move works is duller: the residence permit, how far the nearest serious hospital is, and whether your street still has a bakery open in February. We answer each question below from our research base only, and say plainly where it has nothing.

## Can I get a residence permit by buying a flat?

Yes, ownership is a recognised ground. Article 84 of Law 79/2021 gives a property owner the right to apply for a residence permit, and the law sets no minimum purchase value; agencies describe entry in practice at around €75,000–150,000. The permit is issued for one year and renewed, and permanent residence becomes possible after 5 years. We hold this at medium confidence: confirm it with a migration lawyer before you buy.

Two details matter for readers of this article. First, our sources say that since 2025–26 EU citizens only need an online registration rather than the full permit procedure. That covers German and Austrian buyers; Switzerland is not an EU member, so a Swiss buyer should plan on the owner's permit and ask a lawyer. Second, figures of €100,000 and €300,000 circulate for an "investor permit" after Law 43/2025. We found no official publication behind them, so treat them as unconfirmed.

Our research base documents the ownership route only. If a separate route for pensioners applies to you, that is a question for a migration lawyer; we have no verified facts on it and will not guess.

## What does a seaside flat cost to run each month?

About €93–112 a month for a 60 m² flat with two people, in every coastal city we modelled; in winter it is about €110, of which electricity is roughly €50. That is an estimate built from the 2026 tariffs, not a bill. An empty flat, with the fridge on and nobody home, costs about €45–50 a month.

The parts of that figure:

- **Electricity.** Households pay 8.5 lek per kWh before VAT, 10.2 lek with it, up to 700 kWh a month. Above 700 kWh the whole month is billed at 9.5 lek before VAT (11.4 with it). The average household uses about 230 kWh a month.
- **Heating.** There is no household gas network in Albania. Coastal flats heat with air conditioning: a 12,000 BTU inverter unit run 8 hours a day uses about 145 kWh a month, roughly €16.
- **Water.** Durrës is the most expensive in the country: 120 lek per m³ for water and sewerage together, plus a fixed 200 lek a month. Supply in Durrës and Vlorë runs about 13 hours a day, which is why buildings have roof tanks and pumps. Ask what the building has.
- **Internet.** The market median for fibre is 1,690 lek a month, about €18.
- **Building fee.** Under Law 55/2025 each owner pays a share of the building's expenses. Reported levels are 400–500 lek a month in old blocks, 1,000–1,500 lek in buildings put up after 1993, and up to 3,000 lek in premium blocks. For resort complexes with pools and security we found no reliable figures; ask for the complex's own charge in writing before you sign.
- **Insurance and servicing.** Home insurance starts at 5,000 lek a year, roughly €1–1.5 per m²; servicing an air conditioner is about €65 a unit per year.

## How much is the annual property tax?

Very little today: 0.05% a year of the flat's fiscal value, which is the state reference price per square metre times the area, not the market price. Municipalities can move the result by 30% either way. For a typical 90 m² flat in Tirana that comes to about 3,600–10,200 lek a year. It is collected by the municipality.

A reform is on the table and is not law. The Ministry of Finance draft, consulted until 15 July 2026, would tax housing at 0.1–0.2% of an approximate market value, with a 50% deduction for a first home and an exemption for pensioners living alone, from 2029 for buildings. Until it is enacted, the 0.05% rule applies. Reference prices are their own story in Durrës, where a 2025 draft map was never approved; see [reference prices in Durrës explained](/blog/durres-reference-prices-2026) and our guide to [what tax you will pay](/blog/legal-guide-buyers).

## What does a flat under €100,000 look like in Durrës?

It exists, and the honest way to see it is to look at what is listed today rather than at a brochure. The [Durrës price table](/albania/durres/info) shows what our listings ask district by district, the [monthly asking-price index](/blog/durres-asking-price-index-2026-09) shows how many flats sit in each price band, and [apartments for sale in Durrës](/albania/durres) can be filtered by price. Asking prices are asking prices, not sale prices.

Two checks matter more for a retiree than for an investor. Much of the beach strip was built decades ago and the 2019 earthquake hit it hardest, so ask for the construction year and read [how to check an older building](/blog/durres-old-apartment-earthquake-check). And decide between a flat in the city and one on the beach before you compare prices: [the centre or Plazh](/guides/durres-centre-vs-plazh) are different lives in winter.

## Which coastal towns are still alive in winter?

Vlorë and the city part of Durrës are; most resort strips are not. For retirees our research ranks Vlorë first, because it is a working city with a port, students and hospitals all year, and Sarandë second, for its milder winter but weaker medical care. Winter life is the deciding factor.

- **Durrës.** The centre, Currila and Vollga are lived in all year by local families and people who work in Tirana. The beach zones earn their money in summer; Shkëmbi i Kavajës goes quiet in winter and Golem is a May-to-September market.
- **Vlorë.** The one real city on the southern coast, with winter life that Sarandë and Ksamil do not have.
- **Sarandë.** About 35,000–40,000 people in winter and three to four times that in summer; most flats stand empty in winter and much of the seafront closes in September and October. Ksamil all but shuts.
- **Shëngjin and Velipoja.** The season is about 3 to 3.5 months.

## What do we know about healthcare?

Less than you need, and we would rather say so. Our research records that the state hospital in Sarandë is weak and that serious cases go to Vlorë, Tirana or across to Corfu, and it counts Vlorë's hospitals as a reason to retire there.

In Tirana it lists the Mother Teresa university hospital, the American Hospital and Continental. It holds no verified facts on health insurance for foreign residents or on access to the public system, so settle that with your insurer at home before you move. Life on the coast in winter, including this question, is covered in [what it is like to live on the Riviera](/blog/living-albanian-riviera).

## What should I check in the purchase itself?

The title, yourself. German and Swiss buyers name the same fear in our research: the cadastre is not public, so you cannot look a property up the way you would at home. The answer is to have the ownership extract pulled from the cadastre ASHK independently and to pay nothing before you have it.

A sale is valid only in notarial form, the money goes through the notary's escrow account, a sworn translator is mandatory if you do not speak Albanian, and the whole purchase adds roughly 3–4% to the price. With clean documents it takes 4–8 weeks. Albania has a double-taxation treaty with Germany, among about 46 countries.

## What to do next

1. Decide city first, flat second: spend a week in Vlorë or Durrës in January, not July.
2. Look at [apartments for sale in Durrës](/albania/durres) and at the [price table](/albania/durres/info) to see what your budget meets today.
3. Ask a migration lawyer about your residence route and your insurer about cover abroad.
