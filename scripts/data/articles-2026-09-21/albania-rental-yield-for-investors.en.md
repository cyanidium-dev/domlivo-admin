Rental Yield in Albania for a Foreign Investor: Long Let, Summer Let, Tax and the Manager's Cut

## body
Our research describes the typical Polish buyer in Albania like this: Sarandë, Ksamil or Durrës, a budget of €70,000–150,000, a flat to let to tourists and use for holidays, and an expected return of 10–16% a year, usually heard from a Polish-language intermediary. We read that promise as the top quartile of short lets presented as the average. This article sets out what the data supports, with its confidence, and marks what is missing. None of it is a promise of return; every figure is an estimate or an asking price, and we say which.

## What yield does a long-term let give?

About 4–6% gross in Tirana, 4.5–6% in Durrës, 4–5.5% in Vlorë and 3.5–5% in Sarandë, by our working estimate for 2026. Net yield, after the 15% tax, building fee, insurance, vacancy and a repairs reserve, is typically 1.0–1.5 percentage points lower. The only independent series, Global Property Guide, is from the third quarter of 2022 and gave 6.1% for Tirana and 4.5% for Durrës.

Behind those percentages are asking rents. In Durrës a one-bedroom flat is advertised at €250–400 a month in the centre; a marketplace sample of 18 listings in September 2026 had a median of €390. At the beach the same flat asks €200–350 in winter and €400–600 in summer. We hold these at low-to-medium confidence: the samples are small, and they are what landlords ask.

How a long let works in practice: the deposit is usually 1–2 months' rent, a lease longer than a year is written and notarised, notice is 30–90 days, the tenant pays for consumption, and reported vacancy is 4–8%.

## What does a seasonal let earn?

For a one-bedroom flat let full time and run well, the model estimates are €10,346–12,533 gross a year in Durrës city, at 50–53% occupancy and an average nightly rate of €55–63, and €13,805–17,040 in Sarandë. Golem is a summer market: about €6,800–8,800 from May to September. These are estimates from analytics platforms, before fees, costs and tax, not payouts.

The occupancy caveat is the one brochures skip. Analytics platforms count in two ways. Across all listings, including flats let only part of the year, occupancy is 25–39%; for listings available full time it is 53–62%. Nationally the all-listing figure is 26.5% for the year, 55.7% in August and 13–16% from November to March. If a seller quotes you an occupancy, ask which of the two it is.

Supply is the other caveat. Active listings in Albania rose from 6,342 in April 2020 to 32,229 in April 2026. In 2025 listings in Durrës grew by 69% while revenue per listing rose 2.6%. Hosts who price dynamically reach 44.9% occupancy against 16.5% for those with a fixed price. The return now depends on how the flat is run more than on where it is. City-by-city figures are in [Airbnb income in Albania by city](/blog/short-term-rental-albanian-riviera).

## How is rental income taxed?

At a flat 15%, for residents and non-residents alike. On a long let it is 15% of the gross rent. On a short let through Airbnb or Booking it is 15% of what remains after the platform's commission, and cleaning, utilities and a manager's fee are not deductible. An individual needs no business registration for pure letting, and declares the income in DIVA by 31 March of the following year.

What a Polish owner should add to that:

- A non-resident needs an Albanian tax number to file in DIVA, in practice through a representative.
- Poland is among the roughly 46 countries with a double-taxation treaty: rent from an Albanian flat is taxed in Albania and credited at home. Ask your Polish tax adviser how to report it.
- Our sources report that Booking.com has required a business number (NIPT) from Albanian hosts since 1 November 2025, so the simple individual route fits Airbnb better than Booking. We hold this at medium confidence.
- The municipality charges guests an overnight tax, 140 lek a night in Tirana and 70 lek in Vlorë. We did not retrieve the Durrës amount.
- A draft property-tax reform would treat short-let flats as economic activity, at 0.15–0.25% of market value instead of the housing rate, from 2029. It is not law.

The purchase and ownership taxes are in [what tax you will pay](/blog/legal-guide-buyers).

## What does management cost, and what is left?

No Albanian manager publishes a price list. The reported bands are 10–15% of income for partial service (listing, pricing, guest messages), 20–30% for full service and up to 40–45% for premium firms. Only about 7% of listings, 2,207 of roughly 32,000, are professionally managed. Add the platform: Airbnb takes 3% from the host on its split-fee model or about 15.5% on the host-only model, and Booking about 15%.

Running costs of a short let, including utilities, cleaning and consumables, are reported at €150–200 a month. A professional clean between guests costs an estimated €17–33, more than the median cleaning fee guests pay in any Albanian city, so part of it comes out of the nightly rate.

Put together, our estimate for a Durrës flat grossing €11,000 a year is that the owner keeps about €6,600–7,200 self-managing on Airbnb, and about €3,500–4,100 with a full-service manager at 25% on mixed platforms, both after tax. A manager at that fee pays for itself only if it lifts gross income by about 30% or more. For coastal one-bedroom flats we have no data showing that it does.

## What does it come to on a €100,000 purchase?

In our base case, which assumes self-management, 52% occupancy and a furnished flat, the estimated net yield on the total investment is 6.7% for a short let in Durrës, 7.3% in Sarandë, 4.3% in Tirana and 3.9% in Vlorë; a Golem flat let by the night in summer and by the month in winter comes to 6.0%. An annual contract nets 1.6–3.6% everywhere.

The spread matters more than the middle. With a 25% manager, 45% occupancy and Booking-heavy platform costs, the same purchases fall to 0–2.4% net; the optimistic case reaches 9–10% in Sarandë and Durrës. These are model outputs on asking prices and estimated income. A full worked example is in [a real Durrës rental investment](/blog/durres-rental-investment-2026).

## What is still unknown about Durrës?

Quite a lot. For Durrës there are asking rents from small samples and platform model estimates of short-let income, and nothing else: no published achieved rents, no actual host payouts, no overnight-tax amount and no service charges for resort complexes. A seller who quotes you any of these as fact is guessing. The gaps, one by one:

- **Achieved rents.** No Albanian source publishes what tenants actually pay against what is advertised. Our working assumption is 5–10% below asking, more on the coast in winter.
- **Achieved short-let payouts.** Every income figure above is a platform model; we have no host's or manager's actual payouts to validate them.
- **The overnight tax** in Durrës, and the municipality's 2026 amounts for the building tax.
- **Service charges in resort complexes** with pools and security. We found no reliable figures; ask for them in writing.
- **Managed against self-managed results** for coastal one-bedroom flats.

## faq
**What rental yield can I expect in Albania?**
On an annual contract our working estimate is 4–6% gross in Tirana, 4.5–6% in Durrës, 4–5.5% in Vlorë and 3.5–5% in Sarandë, with net yield typically 1.0–1.5 percentage points lower. Short lets are estimated higher but vary widely: on a €100,000 purchase the modelled net yield in Durrës runs from 2.0% in the conservative case to 9.4% in the optimistic one.

**How is rental income taxed in Albania for a foreign owner?**
At a flat 15%, the same for residents and non-residents. A long let is taxed on the gross rent; a short let through a platform is taxed on income after the platform's commission, and other expenses are not deductible. The income is declared in DIVA by 31 March of the following year, and a non-resident needs an Albanian tax number, usually through a representative.

**How much does an Airbnb manager charge in Albania?**
Managers publish no price lists. Reported bands are 10–15% of income for partial service, 20–30% for full service and up to 40–45% for premium firms, with cleaning usually charged on top. By our estimate a full-service manager at 25% pays for itself only if it lifts gross income by about 30% or more, and only about 7% of listings are professionally managed.

**Is there reliable rental data for Durrës?**
Only partly. Asking rents exist from small samples, such as a median of €390 a month for a one-bedroom flat across 18 listings in September 2026, and short-let income exists as platform model estimates. Achieved rents, actual host payouts, the Durrës overnight tax and service charges in resort complexes are not published anywhere we could find.
