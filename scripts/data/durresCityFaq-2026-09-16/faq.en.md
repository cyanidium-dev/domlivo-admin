**How much does an apartment cost in Durrës in 2026?**
The city average is about €1,450/m², up roughly 18% in a year, but the zones are far apart: €1,900–2,800/m² in the centre and on the Currila–Vollga seafront, €1,200–1,700 in Plazh, €1,100–1,500 on Golem's second and third line, €950–1,200 in Mali i Robit, and €450–800 for old stock in Shkëmbi i Kavajës. **Which area of Durrës is best to buy in?**
It depends on how you will use the flat. To live all year: the centre or Plazh, which are part of the city. For a cheaper summer flat: Golem's second line or Mali i Robit. For a managed resort: Gjiri i Lalzit. For the fastest recent growth: Qerret, up 21–43% in a year. Compare them in [Golem or Plazh](/guides/golem-vs-plazh) and [Qerret or Gjiri i Lalzit](/guides/qerret-vs-gjiri-i-lalzit).

**Is it better to buy near the beach or in the city centre?**
The centre costs more, but it lets all year to local families and Tirana commuters and holds its price out of season. The beach zones are cheaper to enter and earn their money in summer: short-let occupancy in Golem peaks at about 55% in August and falls to about 13% in January. See [the centre or Plazh](/guides/durres-centre-vs-plazh).

**Is buying an apartment to rent out in Durrës a good investment?**
It works as a summer let plus a winter tenancy, not as a year-round short let. Our sources put gross short-let yield in Plazh at 7–11%, the best in Durrës, and we estimate 5–8% for a second- or third-line flat in Golem. Nightly rates are lower than in Vlorë or Sarandë, but so is the entry price, by one and a half to two times.

**What should I check before buying an apartment in Durrës?**
Pull the ownership extract (kartela e pasurisë) from the cadastre ASHK yourself, ask for the occupancy permit (leje përdorimi) of a finished building, compare the floors built with the permit, and get the construction year — the 2019 earthquake hit the beach strip hardest. See our [document checklist](/blog/durres-documents-checklist).

**Are older apartments in Durrës safe after the 2019 earthquake?**
The earthquake damaged more than 14,000 buildings, the worst of it in Plazh and Shkëmbi i Kavajës, and most flats on the beach strip are over 30 years old. An older flat can still be a good buy, but first get its construction year, any post-earthquake assessment and an independent structural engineer's opinion. Read [how to check an older building](/blog/durres-old-apartment-earthquake-check).

**Have reference prices in Durrës changed?**
No. Notary fees and taxes are calculated on the higher of the contract price and the state reference price, and Durrës still uses a single city rate from 2016. A July 2025 draft that would have raised the port zone to 200,000 lek/m² was never approved. See [reference prices in Durrës explained](/blog/durres-reference-prices-2026).

**What costs does a buyer pay on top of the price?**
Budget roughly 3–4% of the price. The notary charges about 0.35% on sales up to 6 million lek, the 2% transfer tax is often shifted to the buyer in practice, registration with the cadastre costs a few thousand lek, and a sworn translator is required if you do not speak Albanian.

**Will the Tirana–Durrës train raise property prices?**
Probably, but in a narrow band. Electric trains are due to run Tirana–Durrës in 22 minutes from early 2027, after repeated delays. Any gain should concentrate within walking distance of the stations, and part of the expectation is already in today's prices. Read [what the train will and won't do](/blog/tirana-durres-train-property-prices).

**Can I buy property in Durrës directly from the owner?**
Yes. Many listings on Domlivo put you in touch with the owner or agent directly, with no commission to us. A sale in Albania is only valid in notarial form, though, so every purchase still goes through a notary, who holds the money and withholds the taxes.
