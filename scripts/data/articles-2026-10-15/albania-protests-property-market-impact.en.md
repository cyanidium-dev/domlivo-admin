Do the Albania Protests Affect Property? What Buyers in Tirana, Durrës and Vlorë Should Know

## excerpt
So far the protests have not shown up in the numbers: prices had already stopped rising before they began, and foreign visitors are still up on last year. What changes is where the risk sits: central Tirana, projects on protected coast and land with a doubtful history. Nine factors, five cities and a checklist for buyers.

## keyFacts
1. The Bank of Albania's housing index was flat in the first half of 2026 against the second half of 2025 and up 10% on a year earlier, down from 28% and 41.7% a year before: the market was cooling before the protests began.
2. Homes took 9.9 months to sell nationwide in the first half of 2026; in Tirana the time grew from 8.1 to 9.7 months, while on the coast it fell from 9.9 to 8.2 months.
3. About 20% of homes sold in the first half of 2026 went to non-residents, and about 85% of those buyers were EU citizens.
4. Foreign visitors to Albania rose 5.3% in January–August 2026 to 9,443,700, although August grew at the slowest pace since the pandemic.
5. The protests are concentrated in central Tirana; the coastal markets of Durrës, Vlorë and Sarandë show no disruption, but projects on protected land and the abolition of the Strategic Investments Law change the risk for off-plan buyers.

## body
Short answer: not yet, and probably not much for ordinary flats. The protests have run since 31 May 2026 and turned violent in Tirana on 13–15 October (see [what is happening and where](/blog/albania-protests-2026-what-is-happening)). The latest official housing figures cover the first half of 2026, so the protest months are not in them yet. What we can do is check every factor that moves a property price and say where the risk has really changed.

## Tirana

The only city where the protests are part of daily life: every evening on the boulevard, around Parliament, the Prime Minister's office and the ministries, close to Blloku. Prices here grew the least, 9% in a year, and the time to sell rose to 9.7 months, so buyers already had room to negotiate before October. Book viewings in the daytime and do not read the evening television as a picture of the whole city. See [apartments for sale in Tirana](/albania/tirana).

## Durrës

The protests reached Durrës in June, when the motorway to Tirana was blocked for one night; we found no reports of clashes in October. Its market runs on summer tourism and foreign buyers, the segment where homes sold fastest in the first half of the year. On Domlivo the median asking price in Durrës is €1,491 per m², against €2,167 in Tirana. See [apartments for sale in Durrës](/albania/durres) and the [price table by district](/albania/durres/info).

## Vlorë

The movement began here, at Zvërnec and the Narta Lagoon, and this is where the outcome matters most. If the Strategic Investments Law goes, large projects on protected coast lose their fast track; that limits new supply along the shore but adds risk for anyone buying off-plan inside or next to a protected area. The city itself, the seafront and the port district, is unaffected. See [property for sale in Vlorë](/albania/vlore).

## Sarandë and the south

No protests have been reported. Demand depends on the tourist season and on foreign buyers, and in October the season is ending as usual. On Domlivo the median asking price in Sarandë is €1,919 per m². See [property for sale in Sarandë](/albania/sarande).

## Shëngjin and the north coast

In February residents of Rrjoll, near Velipojë, protested against a resort built on land they say is theirs. It is a local case, but it is the same lesson as Zvërnec: on this coast the land title matters more than the brochure.

## Can the protests support property prices?

In some ways, yes. The government has promised to abolish the Strategic Investments Law, the EU is watching the protected coast, and Albania closed its first EU membership chapters in July. Less fast-track building on protected shoreline and a stronger anti-corruption push both make existing, properly permitted homes scarcer and safer to own. The risk runs the other way if violence lasts into the 2027 season and tourists or foreign buyers start to stay away.

Our reading of the three scenarios, an assessment and not a forecast:

| Scenario | Signs to watch | Likely effect on property |
|---|---|---|
| Talks and calm | Students accept Rama's offer of talks; rallies shrink | No visible effect; the market stays flat with room to negotiate |
| Protests go on | Rallies continue through the winter, with clashes from time to time | Slower sales in central Tirana; the coast follows tourism, not Tirana |
| Political crisis | Government falls or an early election is called | A pause of a few months in big deals; prices of finished flats hold better than off-plan |

## What to do if you are buying now

1. Keep the protests in proportion: they take up a few streets in central Tirana, mostly in the evening.
2. Check the title history at the state cadastre (ASHK), not only the current owner, especially near protected areas.
3. Avoid off-plan projects that depend on strategic-investment status or sit inside a protected area until the new law is known.
4. Negotiate: 3–5% is normal, more in Tirana, where homes take longer to sell.
5. Pay only by bank transfer through the notary, and in stages on off-plan purchases.
6. If you do not want to travel, buy remotely with a notarised power of attorney.

## faq
**Will property prices in Albania fall because of the protests?**
There is no sign of it so far. The Bank of Albania's index was already flat in the first half of 2026 and up 10% on a year earlier, after growth of 28% and 41.7% in the two previous half-years. The protest months will appear in the figures for the second half of 2026. Our view is that finished flats on the coast are the least exposed, and central Tirana and off-plan projects on protected land the most.

**Is it safe to buy property in Albania now?**
Yes, if you buy the way you would anyway: check the title at the state cadastre, pay only through the notary and the bank, and avoid projects that depend on special fast-track status. The protests are concentrated in central Tirana; we found no reports of notaries, the cadastre or banks closing, and a purchase can be made remotely with a power of attorney.

**Are Durrës and the coast affected by the protests?**
Hardly. In Durrës the protests came down to one night in June when the motorway to Tirana was blocked; we found no reports of clashes in October. On the coast homes sold faster in the first half of 2026, in 8.2 months against 9.9 before, and foreign visitors were up 5.3% in January–August.

**Does the end of the Strategic Investments Law affect ordinary buyers?**
Not directly. The law gave large projects a fast track to permits and state land; ordinary residential buildings never needed it. If you buy off-plan in a big resort on protected coast, ask whether its permits depend on strategic status, and wait for the replacement law if they do.
