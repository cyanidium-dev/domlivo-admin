## body
Albania has seen anti-government protests every evening since 31 May 2026. On 13–15 October, when university and school students joined, they became the most violent days of the movement so far. Below is the short version, a dated timeline, a city-by-city map of where it is happening and what is still unknown. Updated 15 October 2026.

## The short version

- **What:** a decentralised protest movement known as the Flamingo Revolution, named after the flamingos of the Narta Lagoon near Vlorë.
- **Why it started:** a luxury resort planned on the protected coast at Zvërnec by a company linked to Jared Kushner, and a video of private security guards beating a protester there on 30 May.
- **What it wants now:** Prime Minister Edi Rama's resignation, a non-party technical government for 12 months, a new constitution and the repeal of the laws that fast-track "strategic" projects.
- **Where:** mostly central Tirana, every evening on Bulevardi Dëshmorët e Kombit. Earlier also Vlorë, Shkodër, Lezhë and Durrës; in October Korçë and other towns with schools and universities.
- **The government's answer:** Rama says he will not resign, calls the October violence "chaos" and has offered to meet the students.

## The five demands

On 22 June the movement put its demands in writing:

1. The resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama and the whole cabinet.
2. A non-party technical government with a 12-month mandate.
3. A new constitution approved by referendum, with changes to the electoral code and party financing, and a limit of two terms for a prime minister.
4. Repeal of the amendments to the protected-areas and cultural-heritage laws, the "Mountain Package" and the strategic-investments framework.
5. A "new social contract" between citizens and the state.

The students who joined in October add their own list: better conditions at universities, lower fees, financial support and jobs after graduation. "We will not stop until Rama resigns," one student told Reuters.

## What is the Kushner project, and has it been stopped?

The project covers Sazan, an uninhabited island off Vlorë, and the facing coast at Zvërnec, next to the Narta Lagoon, a protected area where flamingos winter. It is linked to Affinity Partners, the investment firm of Jared Kushner, son-in-law of US President Donald Trump. Reports put its cost at about $4 billion; early plans seen by CBS News include 800 rooms and suites, villas and a golf course.

Its status is unclear. In June the environment minister told the European Commission that construction was suspended until an environmental impact assessment is done, while Rama said the project would go on and the assessment would run in parallel. The EU has said the protections at Zvërnec are part of Albania's accession criteria, and the anti-corruption prosecutor SPAK is investigating the land deals. As of 15 October we found no official cancellation.

## Is it safe to visit Albania now?

For most of the country, yes, with one rule: stay away from the protests. The US State Department keeps Albania at level 2, "exercise increased caution", the same level as before the movement; its warning is about crime and adds that demonstrations can start with little notice. The Swiss foreign ministry calls travel to Albania generally safe and notes frequent demonstrations in Tirana and other large cities.

In practice:

- Avoid Bulevardi Dëshmorët e Kombit, Parliament, the Prime Minister's office and the ministries from late afternoon, when the rallies start.
- Expect closed streets and traffic jams in central Tirana in the evening.
- On the coast, in Durrës, Vlorë and Sarandë, life goes on as usual; we found no reports of airport or border closures.

## What happens next

Nobody knows yet. No early election has been called; the last parliamentary election was in May 2025, so the next regular one is due in 2029. Three things to watch: whether the students accept Rama's offer of talks, what law replaces the Strategic Investments Law, and the Bank of Albania's housing figures for the second half of 2026, the first to cover the protest months.

## faq
**What is the Flamingo Revolution in Albania?**
It is the name of the anti-government protest movement that has held rallies in Tirana every evening since 31 May 2026. It began over a luxury resort planned by a company linked to Jared Kushner on the protected coast at Zvërnec, next to the Narta Lagoon and its flamingos, and grew into a movement against corruption that demands the resignation of Prime Minister Edi Rama.

**Why are students protesting in Albania?**
Students joined the movement on 12–13 October 2026. They protest against conditions at universities, high fees, the lack of financial support and the lack of jobs after graduation, and they demand Rama's resignation. Their protests at the Ministry of Education and in central Tirana turned violent on 13–15 October.

**Is it safe to travel to Albania during the protests?**
Outside the protest areas, yes. The protests are concentrated in central Tirana, around the boulevard, Parliament and the ministries, mostly in the evening. The US advisory stays at level 2, "exercise increased caution", and Switzerland calls travel generally safe. We found no reports of clashes on the coast or of airport closures in October.

**Has the Kushner resort in Albania been cancelled?**
No cancellation has been announced as of 15 October 2026. In June the environment minister told the European Commission that construction was suspended pending an environmental assessment, while Prime Minister Rama said the project would continue. Prosecutors are investigating the land deals, and the government has promised to abolish the Strategic Investments Law that fast-tracked it.
