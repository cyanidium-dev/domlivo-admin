## body
How much are utilities in Albania? Less than most buyers from Western Europe expect, and the list is short. We checked the 2026 tariffs against the real bills of an owner in Durrës, who showed them in a [short video](https://youtube.com/shorts/YlIYyuUddco) on the channel "Жизнь в Албании". All amounts in euros are at about 93 lek to the euro, the October 2026 rate.

## What you pay for

- **Electricity:** by the meter, every month. Everything runs on it: air conditioners for heating and cooling, the cooker, the water heater.
- **Water:** by the meter, every month, to the local water company.
- **Internet:** a contract with a provider, monthly or a year in advance.
- **Building administration:** a fee to the building's administrator for the lift, cleaning the stairwell and common areas. In the owner's building in Durrës it is paid once a year and includes a chip for the lift.
- **Property tax:** once a year, to the municipality.

What you do not pay for: central heating, which Albania does not have, and mains gas, which does not exist for homes. Some people install a gas bottle for the cooker, but most flats cook on electricity.

## Real bills: €70–80 a month for electricity and water

The owner in the video pays about €70–80 a month for electricity and water together, plus €100–120 a year for the building and €130–150 a year for internet. That is more than the tariff sums above suggest for an average household, and the reason is the way the flat is used: without gas, the water heater and air conditioners carry the whole load, and a flat heated and cooled by air conditioning all year easily uses twice the average 230 kWh.

How the bills are paid also surprises newcomers. In many buildings each meter has a small booklet: every month the payment is stamped in it and the receipt is kept, one booklet for electricity and one for water.

## How much it costs in total

| Situation | Per month |
|---|---|
| A 60 m² flat, two people, our estimate from 2026 tariffs | €93–112 |
| The Durrës owner, electricity and water only | €70–80 |
| The Durrës owner, internet and building fee spread over the year | about €20 more |
| An empty flat with the fridge on | about €45–50 |

For a landlord this matters twice. On a long let the tenant usually pays the meters; on short lets the owner pays everything, and in summer air conditioning in every room drives the bill. Put utilities into the yield sum before you buy: the advertised yields almost never include them.

## Practical tips

1. Before you buy, ask the seller for the last 12 months of electricity and water bills: they show how the flat really uses energy.
2. Check the type and age of the air conditioners and the water heater: an inverter unit costs about €16 a month to heat a room for 8 hours a day.
3. Ask the administrator for the building fee in writing, and what it covers; in resort complexes with a pool and security it can be several times higher.
4. In Durrës and Vlorë water runs about 13 hours a day, which is why buildings have roof tanks and pumps: ask whether yours has them.

## faq
**How much are utilities in Albania per month?**
For a 60 m² flat with two people, about €93–112 a month in total by our estimate from 2026 tariffs, internet and the building fee included. An owner in Durrës pays about €70–80 a month for electricity and water alone, plus €100–120 a year for the building and €130–150 a year for internet.

**How much does electricity cost in Albania in 2026?**
Households pay 8.5 lek per kWh before VAT, 10.2 lek with it, up to 700 kWh a month. If a month goes over 700 kWh, the whole month is billed at 9.5 lek before VAT, 11.4 lek with it. The average household uses about 230 kWh a month, about 2,350 lek or €25.

**Is there central heating or gas in Albanian flats?**
No. Albania has no central heating and no mains gas for homes. Flats are heated with air conditioners and electric heaters, and most people cook on electric stoves; a few install a gas bottle. That is why electricity is the largest utility bill.

**What is the building fee in Albania?**
Each owner pays a share of the building's costs to its administrator, under Law 55/2025: 400–500 lek a month in old blocks, 1,000–1,500 lek in buildings put up after 1993 and up to 3,000 lek in premium ones. Some buildings bill once a year; in a Durrës building we checked it is €100–120 a year, lift chip included.
