## body
The train will make Durrës a 22-minute commute from Tirana. That is a real change for people who work in the capital and a real argument for Durrës property. But the project is financed and mostly built, which means the market has already priced in part of it, and the dates have slipped often enough that paying for a launch date is a bet, not a fact.

## What is being built

The Tirana–Durrës–Rinas railway reconstructs 34.2 km of line and adds a 5 km branch to Tirana's international airport. Electric multiple-unit trains are planned to cover Tirana–Durrës in 22 minutes. The EBRD is financing about €103–113 million, with grants from the EU's Western Balkans Investment Framework on top.

## Where it stands in 2026

By early 2025 the works were reported at about 80%. The official deadline is December 2026 for construction, with testing and the first services in the first quarter of 2027. The delays so far have come from expropriations and electrification, and the cost has risen by about €40 million. Albanian media have been openly sceptical about the dates; in February 2026 one outlet ran the story under the headline that Tirana–Durrës is again without a train.

## Why it matters for Durrës

Commuting already happens. The SH2 highway takes 30–60 minutes depending on traffic, and 41 buses a day run between the two cities. Renting in Tirana costs about 57% more than in Durrës, so the gap that makes Durrës attractive to people working in Tirana is already there. The train shortens the trip and makes it predictable, which is what turns an occasional commute into a daily one.

## What is already in the price

Our rule for infrastructure is simple. A project that already works is fully in the price. A project that is financed and under construction is about half priced in. An announcement without money should not be bought at all. The railway sits in the middle category: its main upside is still ahead and comes when trains physically run, not when the next deadline is announced.

## What it won't do

It won't lift the whole city. Durrës lost about 21,000 residents between the 2011 and 2023 censuses — its population was 153,614 in 2023 — so local demand is not growing on its own. Any gain will be concentrated where the train changes daily life: within walking distance of the stations along the Tirana–Durrës corridor, including inland districts such as Shkozet, and in the parts of Plazh and Golem that commuters could live in year-round. One market forecast puts that gain at 5–10% a year for homes near stations after launch — a medium-confidence estimate we would not build a purchase around.

It also won't fix what the seaside zones struggle with — the season, the sewerage in Golem, the older stock on the beach strip.

## How to use this as a buyer

- Buy for the use you have today, and treat the train as upside rather than as the reason.
- If commuting to Tirana is the plan, check the walking distance to a station, not the distance to the sea.
- Don't pay a premium a seller justifies with "when the train opens" — ask which year they mean.

## faq
**When will the Tirana–Durrës train open?**
Works are due to finish in December 2026, with testing and the first services planned for the first quarter of 2027. The dates have slipped several times, so treat them as a target rather than a commitment.

**How long will the train take from Tirana to Durrës?**
About 22 minutes on the planned electric trains. Today the SH2 highway takes 30–60 minutes depending on traffic.

**Will the train raise property prices in Durrës?**
Probably in a narrow band: homes within walking distance of stations, once trains actually run. One forecast suggests 5–10% a year there after launch, but it is a medium-confidence estimate, and part of the expectation is already in today's prices.

**Will the train go to Tirana airport?**
Yes. The project includes a 5 km branch to Tirana International Airport at Rinas.
